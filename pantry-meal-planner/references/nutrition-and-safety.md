# Nutrition and safety

## Nutrition calculations

Use a traceable nutrient table whose entries declare values per 100 g edible portion. Calculate each ingredient as:

`nutrient contribution = nutrient_per_100g × edible_grams / 100`

Sum the recipe and divide by actual servings. Keep the table source and calculation date in application data. Cooking, draining, brands, edible yield, and measurement error mean results remain estimates.

Report energy, protein, fat, carbohydrate, fibre, and sodium by default when the source provides them. Never fabricate missing fields. A qualitative description is better than false precision.

## Health boundaries

- General wellness goals such as “more vegetables” or “higher protein” may guide ranking.
- A clinician-provided restriction may be applied as a hard filter, but do not reinterpret or loosen it.
- For diabetes, kidney disease, severe allergies, pregnancy, children, eating disorders, or other high-risk situations, stay conservative and recommend professional review for individualized targets.
- Body measurements alone are not enough to prescribe a therapeutic plan.
- Do not present a single meal as correcting a deficiency or treating a condition.

## Food and allergy safety

- Mention cross-contact risk when an allergy is relevant.
- Treat ambiguous ingredient names and packaged foods as unresolved until labels are checked.
- Avoid definitive safety claims when storage time or temperature is unknown.
- Recommend discarding food when the user describes clear spoilage or unsafe storage; do not advise tasting it.
- For raw animal foods, provide a reliable doneness method appropriate to the context, preferably a thermometer when available.

End health-sensitive answers with a proportionate note, not a generic alarming disclaimer. Explain the specific uncertainty that matters.
